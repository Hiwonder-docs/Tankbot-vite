# 1. Getting Started

## 1.1 Understanding Tankbot

### 1.1.1 Product Introduction

Tankbot is an intelligent transport robot built on ESP32 controller and STM32 controller. Its tank chassis carries a 6-DOF robotic arm equipped with intelligent bus servos. The robot supports multiple control methods including app control, wireless controller control, and somatosensory glove control, and can perform various gripping and transport functions. With the included sensor modules, it enables diverse creative applications.

## 1.2 Servo and Controller Introduction

### 1.2.1 Bus Servo Introduction

Bus servos are derived from digital servos but differ from traditional PWM digital servos in that they communicate via an asynchronous serial bus. Control is achieved through the transmission and reception of command packets, representing a closed-loop control form. Based on this characteristic, multiple bus servos can be daisy-chained, simplifying wiring and reducing UART port usage.

Therefore, just as people are given names, the connection between bus servos requires the ID of each servo to be set in advance. Otherwise, individual servos cannot be distinguished during control. In this way, during communication, commands such as "Servo ID1, rotate 30 degrees. Servo ID2, rotate 40 degrees..." can be sent to the bus servos.

The bus servo employs a high-precision potentiometer internally. Compared with PWM servo control, it not only provides feedback on position, temperature, and voltage but also delivers excellent precision and linearity. This results in more stable robot operation and significantly extends servo service life.

Additionally, the servo must be centered prior to assembly.

The central position of a servo is its initial position, which serves as the zero point for positive and negative angle rotation. Therefore, the servo must be adjusted to the central position before being mounted onto the servo horn.

This is because when the servo rotates, the rotating parts drive the potentiometer to rotate. The software assumes the central position as the zero point. Otherwise, the potentiometer may enter a blind zone, causing the entire component to fail to operate normally. This may further result in the specified angle not being reached or the corresponding action groups being inconsistent during robot use.

> [!NOTE]
>
> * **All servos on the Tankbot have their corresponding IDs preset and central positions calibrated at the factory, so no further operation is required. For later debugging or learning, the relevant information can be found in 4. Extended Learning Materials\Bus Servo Settings.**
>
> * **The bus servo communication protocol can also be found in the Basic Development Course\Bus Servo Control folder within each main controller programming course.**

### 1.2.4 Core Board Introduction

* **ESP32 controller**

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_2/media/image3.png"   width="200px" />

The main control chip of this development board is the ESP32-WROOM, with an onboard Type-C interface for program download and serial communication. The left button is the IO0 button, which can control the level of the corresponding pin and can be customized by the user program to trigger event execution. The right button is the EN reset button, which can achieve hardware reset functionality when pressed.

* **STM32 controller**

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_2/media/image4.png"  width="200px"   />

The main control chip of this development board is STM32F103RBT6, and the board is equipped with a Type-C interface for program download and serial communication. The BOOT button on the left is connected to the Boot 0 pin of the chip, and it can be pressed during firmware programming to select the download mode. The RST button on the right can be pressed to perform a hardware reset.

## 1.3 Hands-on Assembly

### 1.3.1 Assembly Instructions

Tankbot is shipped as a semi-finished product and must be assembled before use. The assembly procedure is described below.

> [!Note]
>
> **Upon receiving the robotic arm, connect the bus servos in series using a 3PIN cable.**

1) Install the controller:

Use four M3 × 6+6 single-pass nylon standoffs and four M3 × 6 round-head screws to secure the controller to the tank chassis.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image1.png"   width="600px" />

Then install the power cable and the USB wireless controller receiver onto the controller.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image2.png"   width="600px" />

2) Install the 4-channel motor driver module:

Use four M4 × 6+6 single-pass nylon standoffs and four M4 × 6 round-head screws to secure the 4-channel motor driver module to the chassis.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image3.png"   width="600px" />

Connect the 200 mm 3-pin cable and the 200 mm 4-pin cable to the controller. The 3-pin cable is used for power supply, and the 4-pin cable is used for I2C communication.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image4.png"   width="600px" />

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image5.png"   width="600px" />

Finally, connect the motor wires to the M1 and M3 terminals on the 4-channel motor driver module respectively.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image21.png"   width="600px" />



3) Install the 8-channel line follower sensor:

Use M4 × 6+6 single-ended nylon standoffs, M4 screws, and M4 × 6 button-head screws to secure the 8-channel line follower sensor to the chassis.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image6.png"   width="600px" />

Then connect the controller to the IIC interface of the 8-channel line follower sensor using a 4-pin cable of 400 mm.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image7.png"   width="600px" />

4) Install the battery:

Use cable ties to secure the battery to the battery cover.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image8.png"   width="600px" />

5) Install the robotic arm:

Secure the robotic arm to the tank chassis using four M3 × 10 countersunk head screws.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image9(2).png"   width="600px" />

Then connect bus servo ID6 to the controller using a 3-pin cable.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image10.png"   width="600px" />

6) Install the RGB ultrasonic sensor module:

Use M4 × 6 pan head screws to secure the glowy ultrasonic sensor to the robotic arm.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image11(1).png"   width="600px" />

Then use a 4-pin cable with a length of 300 mm to connect the RGB ultrasonic sensor to the controller.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image12.png"   width="600px" />

7) Install the large AI model module:

Use M4 × 12 round-head screws and M4 nuts to secure the large model bracket to the robotic arm, then use two M3 × 6 round-head screws to secure the large model module to the bracket.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image13.png"   width="600px" />

Then use a 500 mm 4-pin cable to connect the large model module to the controller.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_3/media/image14.png"   width="600px" />

### 1.3.3 Charging Instructions

Since lithium batteries cannot be fully charged during transportation, charge the battery before initial use for approximately 1 hour.

The charging procedure is as follows:

1) Ensure the device switch is in the **OFF** position.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_5/media/image1.png"  width="700px" />

2) Insert the charger into the DC port of the lithium battery. The red indicator light indicates charging, and the green indicator light indicates that charging is complete.

<img class="common_img" src="../_static/media/chapter_1/section_5/media/image2.png" width="700px"  />

### 1.3.4 Battery Usage Specification

1) Use the 8.4 V charger included with the kit to charge the battery.

2) The adapter indicator lights red during charging and green when fully charged. Disconnect the power promptly after full charge to avoid overcharging the battery.

3) If the robot is expected to be unused for an extended period, fully charge the battery, set the switch on the expansion board to the **OFF** position, remove the battery, and store it in a cool and dry place.

**Declaration: The company shall not assume any liability for product damage, economic losses, safety accidents, or any other consequences arising from failure to use the product in accordance with the specifications in this manual.**



### 1.3.5 Device Usage Precautions

1) After power-on, do not forcibly turn the servos to avoid damage.

2) Do not keep the servos at the limit position for an extended period to avoid servo stall.

3) Do not place fingers near the joint sections of the robotic arm to avoid pinching injuries.

4) The servos of the robotic arm are precision components and consumables, requiring replacement after prolonged or excessive use.

5) Please use the supporting Wonderbot app for connection. Do not pair via keys in the phone settings.



### 1.3.6 First Power-On

1) Push the power switch to **ON**.  
2) After the robotic arm on Tankbot returns to its initial posture, the power-on is successful.
